How Maryland Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Maryland taxes all short-term and long-term capital gains as ordinary income in 2026 under its 2%–5.75% state brackets PLUS Maryland's mandatory County Local Income Tax (2.25% to 3.20% in Montgomery, Howard, Prince George's, and Baltimore City)—creating a true combined Maryland state + county capital gains rate of roughly 7.95% to 8.95%!

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Maryland State Tax

On a $100,000 long-term stock or crypto gain for a Single filer earning $150,000 in Bethesda (Montgomery County) or Baltimore, Maryland collects roughly $4,750 in state tax PLUS $3,200 in mandatory 3.2% county tax = $7,950—bringing total combined tax (Federal 15% + NIIT + MD) to ~$24,090 (24.1% effective). That is roughly $2,200/year higher per $100k gain than living just across the Potomac River in Northern Virginia (5.75% cap, zero county income tax).

4 Legal Ways Maryland Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Remember Maryland's 2.25%–3.20% county tax applies to capital gains too—always factor ~8%–9% combined state + county tax into your quarterly estimated payments.
  • Harvest capital losses before December 31: because Maryland starts from Federal AGI, every dollar of loss cuts Federal, Maryland State, AND Maryland County taxes all at once!
  • Use Donor-Advised Funds (DAFs) to gift appreciated long-term stock and avoid ~27%–32.75% combined federal + Maryland + county capital gains tax.
  • For Maryland investment properties, use a 1031 Exchange to defer federal LTCG, 25% depreciation recapture, 3.8% NIIT, and Maryland's ~8%–8.95% combined state + county tax.

Frequently Asked Questions: Maryland Capital Gains Tax

Does Maryland's county local income tax apply to stock and crypto capital gains?

Yes. All 23 Maryland counties and Baltimore City apply their local piggyback tax (2.25%–3.20%) to realized capital gains on top of Maryland's 2%–5.75% state rate.

Does Maryland have a lower tax rate for long-term capital gains?

No. Maryland taxes short-term and long-term capital gains identically as ordinary income.

Does Maryland conform to the federal $250k/$500k home sale exclusion?

Yes. Primary home gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are 100% exempt from both Maryland state and county taxes.
