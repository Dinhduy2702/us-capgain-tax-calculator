How New Jersey Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

New Jersey taxes all capital gains as ordinary Gross Income in 2026 with progressive brackets running from 1.4% up to 10.75% on income over $1,000,000. Just like California and New York, New Jersey offers zero discounted rate for long-term investments held longer than one year. Even more importantly, New Jersey enforces one of the harshest capital loss rules in the country: unlike the IRS, New Jersey does NOT allow you to deduct $3,000 of net capital losses against W-2 wages or carry unused capital losses forward to future tax years.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & New Jersey State Tax

Because New Jersey disallows capital loss carryforwards, timing your Tax-Loss Harvesting is far more unforgiving for NJ residents than for federal filers. If you realize a $30,000 stock loss in 2026 and have $0 capital gains in 2026, that $30,000 loss disappears permanently on your New Jersey state return—even though the IRS lets you carry it forward forever. Additionally, New Jersey charges a well-known 'Exit Tax' (estimated tax withholding at closing) when non-residents sell New Jersey real estate, though qualifying primary home sellers still benefit from the federal $250k/$500k Section 121 exclusion.

4 Legal Ways New Jersey Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Match realized capital losses with realized capital gains inside the EXACT SAME calendar year in New Jersey—never leave large net capital losses unused at Dec 31 if you hold winning positions you can step up.
  • Remember New Jersey has no Wash-Sale rule on gains: if you have a $20,000 net loss in December, harvest $20,000 of gains from a winning ETF and immediately rebuy it to lock in a higher NJ cost basis.
  • Use a 1031 Tax-Deferred Exchange for New Jersey rental properties (NJ fully conforms to federal 1031 rules) to defer both federal and NJ 6.37%–10.75% taxes.
  • For high earners commuting to NYC, coordinate your NJ resident credit for NY taxes paid so you are not double-taxed on wage income while planning brokerage sales.

Frequently Asked Questions: New Jersey Capital Gains Tax

Can I carry forward unused capital losses on my New Jersey state tax return?

No. New Jersey is one of the very few states that prohibits carrying forward net capital losses to future years and does not allow the $3,000 ordinary income deduction.

What is New Jersey's real estate 'Exit Tax'?

It is not an extra tax—it is a mandatory estimated tax withholding (minimum 2% of sale price or 8.97%–10.75% of gain) collected at closing when selling NJ property and moving out of state; overpayments are refunded when you file your NJ return.

Does New Jersey tax long-term capital gains at a lower rate?

No. Both short-term and long-term gains are taxed at NJ ordinary income brackets up to 10.75%.
