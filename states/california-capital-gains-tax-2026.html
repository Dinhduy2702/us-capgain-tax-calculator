How California Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

California levies the highest state capital gains tax rate in the United States in 2026. Unlike the federal IRS system, the California Franchise Tax Board (FTB) offers zero preferential rate for long-term capital gains. Whether you hold Nvidia stock, Bitcoin, or an investment property for three weeks or ten years, California taxes 100% of your realized gain under its progressive ordinary income brackets ranging from 1% up to 12.3%, plus an additional 1% Mental Health Services Tax on taxable income above $1,000,000 (bringing the statutory top capital gains rate to 13.3%).

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & California State Tax

When you stack California's top 13.3% rate on top of the 2026 federal 20% long-term capital gains bracket and the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT), high-earning California investors face a combined 37.1% marginal rate on long-term profits and over 50% on short-term trades. Even a middle-to-upper-income Single filer earning $150,000 in W-2 salary sits in California's 9.3% marginal bracket—meaning every $100,000 of stock or crypto gain triggers roughly $9,300 in state tax before federal LTCG and NIIT are even counted. Furthermore, California does not conform to federal Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS Section 1202) exclusions, making pre-sale tax planning critical for Bay Area and Silicon Beach founders.

4 Legal Ways California Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Pair Federal Tax-Loss Harvesting with California gains before December 31—California allows dollar-for-dollar loss offsets plus up to $3,000/yr against ordinary wages.
  • Maximize Section 121 Primary Home Exclusions ($250,000 Single / $500,000 Married) and document every capital improvement receipt (roof, kitchen, ADU) to raise cost basis.
  • Use a 1031 Tax-Deferred Exchange for California rental properties, but note California's strict 'clawback' tracking rule (FTB Form 3840) if you exchange into an out-of-state property.
  • Spread large concentrated stock or RSU sales across two tax years (December and January) to avoid pushing gains into the 11.3%–13.3% top tiers and the 3.8% NIIT zone.

Frequently Asked Questions: California Capital Gains Tax

Does California have a lower tax rate for long-term capital gains in 2026?

No. California taxes both short-term and long-term capital gains at the exact same ordinary income tax rates, topping out at 13.3% on income above $1 million.

Does California conform to the federal $250k/$500k home sale exclusion?

Yes. California generally conforms to IRS Section 121, allowing qualifying homeowners who lived in their primary residence for 2 of the last 5 years to exclude up to $250,000 (Single) or $500,000 (Married) of gain.

How does California's 1031 Exchange clawback rule work?

If you defer California real estate gains via a 1031 Exchange into a replacement property in a 0%-tax state like Texas or Florida, California tracks the deferred gain via Form 3840 and collects its share whenever you eventually sell without another 1031.
