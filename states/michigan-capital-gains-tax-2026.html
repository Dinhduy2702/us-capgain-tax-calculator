How Michigan Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Michigan taxes both short-term and long-term capital gains at its flat 4.25% state income tax rate in 2026. While most Michigan suburbs (Ann Arbor, Troy, Novi, Royal Oak) charge 0% local tax on capital gains, residents living inside the City of Detroit (2.4%) or Grand Rapids (1.5%) do owe local city resident tax on capital gains—whereas non-residents commuting into Detroit do NOT owe Detroit city tax on personal stock or crypto gains.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Michigan State Tax

On a $100,000 long-term stock or crypto gain in suburban Michigan, the state bill is a predictable $4,250—bringing total combined tax (Federal 15% + 3.8% NIIT + MI 4.25%) to roughly ~$20,390 (20.4% effective). Because Michigan begins from Federal AGI, federal Tax-Loss Harvesting, $3,000 ordinary loss offsets, loss carryforwards, Section 121 primary home exclusions, and 1031 Exchanges all flow cleanly into your Michigan return.

4 Legal Ways Michigan Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Use December Tax-Loss Harvesting to cut both federal and Michigan 4.25% taxes dollar-for-dollar.
  • Remember Detroit non-residents do NOT pay Detroit city tax on personal brokerage capital gains—only Detroit residents do.
  • Hold winning stocks and crypto past 366 days to capture federal 0%/15%/20% LTCG brackets while Michigan stays flat at 4.25%.
  • For Michigan rental properties, combine a 1031 Exchange with federal depreciation planning to defer both federal and MI 4.25% taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions: Michigan Capital Gains Tax

What is Michigan's capital gains tax rate in 2026?

Michigan taxes all capital gains at its flat 4.25% state income tax rate (plus local city tax only if you live inside a taxing city like Detroit or Grand Rapids).

Does Michigan allow the $3,000 capital loss deduction and carryforward?

Yes. Because Michigan starts with Federal AGI, all federal capital loss offsets and carryforwards flow straight through.

Does Michigan conform to the federal $250k/$500k home sale exclusion?

Yes. Gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are 100% tax-free in Michigan.
