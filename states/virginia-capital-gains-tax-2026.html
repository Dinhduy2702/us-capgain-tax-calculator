How Virginia Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Virginia taxes both short-term and long-term capital gains as ordinary state income in 2026, with a top marginal rate of 5.75% that kicks in at just $17,000 of taxable income. Because the top bracket threshold is so low, virtually all full-time earners and investors in Northern Virginia, Richmond, and Hampton Roads effectively pay a flat 5.75% state tax on realized stock, crypto, and real estate gains—with zero local city or county income tax added on top.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Virginia State Tax

While Virginia does not offer a special reduced rate for long-term investments, its 5.75% ceiling is less than half of California's 13.3% and notably lower than neighboring Washington, D.C. (up to 10.75%) and Maryland (up to 8.95% combined state + county tax). For a Northern Virginia defense/tech professional earning $150,000 and realizing a $100,000 long-term stock gain, Virginia's tax bill is $5,750—bringing the combined federal + NIIT + Virginia effective rate to roughly 21.9%. Virginia also conforms cleanly to Federal AGI, preserving all federal Tax-Loss Harvesting, Section 121 primary home exclusions, and 1031 Exchange benefits.

4 Legal Ways Virginia Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Maximize federal holding periods (> 1 year) since Virginia adds a flat 5.75% regardless of term, making federal LTCG vs. STCG your primary tax-saving lever.
  • Take advantage of Virginia's full conformity to Federal AGI: harvest losses before December 31 to trim both federal and 5.75% Virginia tax bills simultaneously.
  • For Northern Virginia homeowners who bought before 2020, document every renovation receipt carefully so your gain stays comfortably inside the $250k/$500k Section 121 exclusion.
  • Investors holding Virginia rental properties can execute a 1031 Exchange to defer federal LTCG, 25% depreciation recapture, 3.8% NIIT, and Virginia's 5.75% tax.

Frequently Asked Questions: Virginia Capital Gains Tax

Do Northern Virginia counties (Fairfax, Arlington, Loudoun) charge local capital gains tax?

No. Unlike neighboring Maryland, Virginia counties and cities do not levy local personal income or capital gains taxes—only the 5.75% state rate applies.

Does Virginia have a special lower rate for long-term capital gains?

No. Both short-term and long-term gains are taxed at Virginia's 5.75% top rate once total taxable income exceeds $17,000.

Is there any special Virginia exemption for long-term capital gains?

Virginia offers a narrow subtraction only for certain long-term gains tied to investments in qualified Virginia-based technology or bioscience businesses—standard public stocks and crypto are taxed at 5.75%.
