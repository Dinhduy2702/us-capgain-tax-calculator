How Oregon Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Oregon levies one of the highest state capital gains tax rates in the United States in 2026, taxing both short-term and long-term capital gains as ordinary income up to 9.9% (which kicks in at just $125,000 Single / $250,000 Married!). On top of Oregon's 9.9% state rate, residents of Portland / Multnomah County with taxable income above $125k Single ($200k Married) face an extra 1.0% Metro Supportive Housing tax and 1.5%–3.0% Multnomah Preschool tax—pushing combined top long-term capital gains rates in Portland past 36%!

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Oregon State Tax

On a $100,000 long-term stock or crypto gain for a Single filer earning $150,000 in Oregon, 100% of the gain sits in Oregon's 9.9% top bracket—costing $9,900 in Oregon state tax (even higher than California's $9,300 at the same $150k income level!) plus $15,000 federal LTCG and $1,140 NIIT = $26,040 total. That makes Tax-Loss Harvesting, Donor-Advised Funds, and multi-year sale splitting critical for Portland and Bend investors.

4 Legal Ways Oregon Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Split large stock or crypto sales across December and January to keep Portland household income under the $125k Single / $200k Married local surtax cliffs (saving 2.5%+ instantly).
  • Harvest capital losses aggressively before December 31—Oregon conforms to Federal AGI, so every dollar of loss offsets 9.9% Oregon tax + federal tax simultaneously.
  • Donate appreciated long-term stock via a Donor-Advised Fund (DAF) instead of cash to wipe out both federal and Oregon 9.9% capital gains tax.
  • For Oregon rental properties, execute a 1031 Exchange to defer federal LTCG, 25% depreciation recapture, 3.8% NIIT, and Oregon's 9.9% state tax.

Frequently Asked Questions: Oregon Capital Gains Tax

Does Oregon have a lower rate for long-term capital gains in 2026?

No. Oregon taxes short-term and long-term capital gains identically as ordinary income, hitting 9.9% above $125,000 Single ($250,000 Married).

Do Portland's local Metro and Preschool taxes apply to capital gains?

Yes! Both the 1.0% Metro Supportive Housing tax and 1.5%–3.0% Multnomah County Preschool for All tax apply to taxable capital gains above $125k Single / $200k Married.

Does Oregon conform to the federal $250k/$500k home sale exclusion?

Yes. Primary home gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are 100% exempt from Oregon state and Portland local taxes.
