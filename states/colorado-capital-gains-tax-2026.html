How Colorado Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Colorado levies one of the lowest flat state income tax rates in the nation at 4.4% in 2026 (subject to minor temporary TABOR surplus reductions in qualifying years). Every dollar of realized capital gain—whether from Denver tech stock, crypto, or Front Range real estate—is taxed at the exact same 4.4% flat state rate, with zero local municipal income taxes anywhere in Colorado.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Colorado State Tax

Because Colorado starts directly from Federal Taxable Income, investors enjoy seamless conformity with federal 0%/15%/20% loss offsets, $3,000 ordinary deductions, loss carryforwards, Section 121 primary home exclusions ($250k/$500k), and 1031 Exchanges. On a $100,000 long-term capital gain for a Single filer earning $150,000, Colorado collects just $4,400—saving nearly $5,000 compared to California and over $2,400 compared to New York State. Combined with low property tax rates relative to Texas or Illinois, Colorado remains a highly tax-efficient state for both equity investors and real estate landlords.

4 Legal Ways Colorado Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Time large equity or crypto sales around federal 20% LTCG and 3.8% NIIT cliffs ($200k Single / $250k Married), since Colorado's 4.4% rate stays completely flat.
  • Pair December Tax-Loss Harvesting with federal offsets to simultaneously shave 4.4% off your Colorado state tax bill.
  • If you own Colorado real estate held for 5+ years, verify whether your asset qualifies for Colorado's narrow in-state real property capital gain subtraction (capped and restricted to qualifying agricultural/business property).
  • Use a 1031 Tax-Deferred Exchange when upgrading Denver or mountain rental properties to keep 100% of your federal and 4.4% Colorado tax equity compounding.

Frequently Asked Questions: Colorado Capital Gains Tax

What is the Colorado capital gains tax rate in 2026?

Colorado taxes all capital gains at its flat 4.4% state income tax rate (occasionally reduced slightly in years with TABOR revenue refunds).

Does Denver charge a local city tax on stock or real estate capital gains?

No. Denver's Occupational Privilege Tax is a tiny flat monthly payroll fee ($5.75/mo)—there is zero local city tax on capital gains in Colorado.

Does Colorado conform to the federal $250k/$500k home sale exclusion?

Yes. Because Colorado starts with Federal Taxable Income, gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are automatically 100% tax-free in Colorado as well.
