How Illinois Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Illinois applies a simple, constitutionally mandated flat 4.95% state income tax to all realized capital gains in 2026—regardless of your total income level or whether you held the asset for one day or twenty years. There are no progressive state brackets to climb and no local Chicago city income tax on capital gains, making Illinois significantly more predictable (and less punitive for high earners) than coastal states like California, New York, or New Jersey.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Illinois State Tax

For a Chicago tech or finance professional realizing a $100,000 long-term stock gain on top of a $150,000 salary, Illinois adds a flat $4,950 state bill—less than half of California's $9,300+ hit. Because Illinois begins its state tax calculation directly from Federal Adjusted Gross Income (AGI), Illinois residents automatically inherit federal Tax-Loss Harvesting offsets, the $3,000 annual ordinary loss deduction, indefinite loss carryforwards, Section 121 primary home exclusions ($250k/$500k), and 1031 Exchange deferrals. Even better, Illinois completely exempts retirement income (401(k), IRA, and pension distributions) from its 4.95% state tax.

4 Legal Ways Illinois Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Prioritize maxing 401(k) and Traditional IRA contributions in Illinois—not only do you get federal deductions today, Illinois taxes 0% of qualified retirement withdrawals later.
  • Because Illinois is a flat 4.95%, focus multi-year sale splitting primarily on staying below federal 20% LTCG and 3.8% NIIT thresholds ($200k Single / $250k Married).
  • Take full advantage of federal Tax-Loss Harvesting and loss carryforwards since Illinois flows directly from Federal AGI.
  • For Illinois rental properties, pair a 1031 Exchange with federal depreciation rules to defer both the 4.95% state hit and 25% federal depreciation recapture.

Frequently Asked Questions: Illinois Capital Gains Tax

Does Chicago charge a local city tax on stock or crypto capital gains?

No. Neither Chicago nor any other Illinois municipality levies a local personal income or capital gains tax—you only pay the flat 4.95% state rate plus federal tax.

Are capital gains inside a 401(k) or IRA taxed when withdrawn in Illinois?

No. Illinois exempts federally taxed retirement distributions (401(k), Traditional IRA, pension, Social Security) from state income tax.

Does Illinois allow the $3,000 capital loss deduction and carryforward?

Yes. Because Illinois starts with Federal AGI, your federal net capital loss deduction (up to $3,000/yr) and carryforwards flow straight through.
