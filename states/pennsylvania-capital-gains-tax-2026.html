How Pennsylvania Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Pennsylvania charges one of the lowest flat capital gains tax rates in the United States at just 3.07% in 2026—and critically, local PA municipal Earned Income Taxes (including Philadelphia's 3.75% wage tax!) do NOT apply to capital gains! However, Pennsylvania enforces a famous tax trap similar to New Jersey: Pennsylvania does NOT allow you to deduct $3,000 of net capital losses against W-2 wages OR carry unused capital losses forward to future tax years!

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Pennsylvania State Tax

On a $100,000 long-term stock or crypto gain, a Pennsylvania resident pays only $3,070 in PA state tax—bringing total combined tax (Federal 15% + NIIT + PA 3.07%) to just ~$19,210 (19.2% effective), even inside Philadelphia or Pittsburgh. But because PA disallows capital loss carryforwards, realizing a $25,000 stock loss in a year with $0 capital gains wastes the Pennsylvania state tax benefit permanently—so PA investors must pair losses and gains inside the EXACT SAME calendar year.

4 Legal Ways Pennsylvania Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Match realized capital losses with realized gains inside the SAME calendar year in Pennsylvania—never leave large net losses stranded at Dec 31 if you hold winning ETFs you can step up.
  • Remember Philadelphia's 3.75% city wage tax does NOT tax investment capital gains (only earned wages)—making stock/ETF appreciation much more tax-efficient than W-2 bonuses in Philly.
  • Note that Pennsylvania recently conformed to federal 1031 Like-Kind Exchange deferral rules (previously PA taxed 1031 exchanges!), unlocking full deferral for PA real estate investors.
  • Keep full records of home improvements alongside the $250k/$500k Section 121 primary home exclusion, which PA conforms to cleanly.

Frequently Asked Questions: Pennsylvania Capital Gains Tax

Does Philadelphia's 3.75% city wage tax apply to stock or crypto capital gains?

No! Philadelphia's wage tax applies only to earned wages/salaries, NOT to capital gains from selling stocks, crypto, or real estate—you only pay Pennsylvania's flat 3.07% state rate + federal tax.

Can I carry forward unused capital losses on my Pennsylvania PA-40 return?

No. Pennsylvania prohibits carrying net capital losses forward to future years and does not allow deducting $3,000 of capital losses against W-2 wages.

Does Pennsylvania recognize 1031 Exchanges in 2026?

Yes! Pennsylvania enacted conformity to federal IRS Section 1031 exchanges, allowing PA investment property owners to defer PA's 3.07% tax alongside federal taxes.
