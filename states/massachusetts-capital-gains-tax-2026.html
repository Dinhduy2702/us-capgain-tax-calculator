How Massachusetts Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Massachusetts is one of the rare states that explicitly separates Short-Term and Long-Term Capital Gains rates in 2026. Long-Term Capital Gains (assets held over 1 year) are taxed at a flat 5.0%, while Short-Term Capital Gains (assets held 1 year or less) face a steeper 8.5% state rate (recently reduced from 12% under Massachusetts tax reform). On top of those base rates, the Massachusetts 'Fair Share Amendment' (Millionaire Surtax) adds an extra 4.0% tax on annual taxable income exceeding the ~$1 million inflation-adjusted threshold—pushing ultra-high-income LTCG to 9.0% and STCG to 12.5%.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Massachusetts State Tax

That 3.5-percentage-point state gap between 8.5% Short-Term and 5.0% Long-Term makes holding past 366 days doubly rewarding for Boston biotech and tech investors. On a $100,000 stock gain, waiting for Long-Term status saves $3,500 in Massachusetts state tax ALONE—on top of $10,000+ in federal tax savings! Homeowners selling high-value Greater Boston real estate should also watch the $1 million Fair Share surtax closely: if your taxable gain after the $250k/$500k Section 121 exclusion plus your regular salary exceeds ~$1.08M in a single year, the excess gets hit with an extra 4% Massachusetts surtax.

4 Legal Ways Massachusetts Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Never sell a winning position at Day 330 in Massachusetts without running the Wait-vs-Sell Reverse check—you save BOTH federal ordinary-vs-LTCG spread AND 3.5% in state tax.
  • If your household income + capital gain is near the ~$1M Millionaire Surtax line, split stock sales across December and January to stay below the 4% surtax threshold.
  • Remember Massachusetts limits capital loss deductions against interest/dividends to $2,000/yr (separate from federal $3,000 rules), so prioritize offsetting actual capital gains.
  • Consider a 1031 Exchange or installment sale for Massachusetts investment properties to keep annual taxable income under the 4% Fair Share surtax.

Frequently Asked Questions: Massachusetts Capital Gains Tax

Why is Massachusetts short-term capital gains tax higher than long-term?

Massachusetts taxes short-term gains (<= 1 year) at 8.5% to discourage short-term speculation, while taxing long-term gains (> 1 year) at the standard 5.0% rate.

Does the 4% Massachusetts Millionaire Surtax apply to selling my home?

Yes, if your TAXABLE gain (after subtracting cost basis, improvements, and the $250k/$500k Section 121 exclusion) plus other income pushes your total MA taxable income over the ~$1M inflation-adjusted threshold.

Can married couples file separately in Massachusetts to avoid the 4% surtax?

Massachusetts closed that loophole—married couples must generally use the same filing status on their MA return as on their federal return.
