How Florida Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Florida imposes 0% state capital gains tax in 2026 on stocks, ETFs, crypto assets, retirement distributions, and real estate sales. Like Texas, Florida's constitution forbids a personal state income tax, making it the premier destination for retirees, high-net-worth Wall Street executives, and remote founders liquidating appreciated equity. Florida residents only pay federal IRS capital gains taxes (0%, 15%, or 20% long-term) plus the 3.8% NIIT surtax above $200k/$250k MAGI.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Florida State Tax

The tax arbitrage between New York City and Miami is dramatic: on $250,000 of realized long-term stock gains, a Florida resident saves roughly $25,000 to $36,000 in state and city taxes compared to a New York resident. Florida also pairs its 0% capital gains policy with the 'Save Our Homes' homestead cap (limiting annual assessed value increases on primary residences to 3%) and zero state estate or inheritance tax. That makes Florida uniquely favorable for both active portfolio rebalancing and generational real estate wealth.

4 Legal Ways Florida Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

Frequently Asked Questions: Florida Capital Gains Tax

Is there any Florida state tax on selling crypto or stocks in 2026?

No. Florida has 0% personal income tax and 0% state capital gains tax on stocks, crypto, options, and real estate.

Do Florida snowbirds still owe capital gains tax to New York or New Jersey?

If you remain domiciled in NY/NJ or spend 183+ days there with a permanent abode, your northern state can still tax your capital gains. True Florida domicile is required for 0% state tax.

Does Florida charge a real estate transfer tax when I sell my home?

Florida does not tax your capital gain, though sellers typically pay a small county documentary stamp tax ($0.70 per $100 of sale price in most counties, $0.60 in Miami-Dade) at closing.
