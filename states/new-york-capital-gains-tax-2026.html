How New York Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

New York taxes all capital gains—both short-term and long-term—as ordinary state income in 2026, with zero preferential rate for assets held longer than one year. New York State progressive brackets climb from 4% up to 10.9% for ultra-high earners, and residents of New York City pay an additional city income tax of up to 3.876% on their capital gains. Combined with federal 20% LTCG and the 3.8% NIIT, Manhattan and Brooklyn investors can face a top combined long-term capital gains rate of 38.58%—the highest in the nation.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & New York State Tax

For a Single filer earning $150,000 in W-2 income in New York State, an extra $100,000 stock or crypto gain lands in the ~6.0%–6.85% state bracket (plus ~3.876% if living in NYC). On top of state and local capital gains taxes, New York real estate sellers must budget for state transfer taxes and, for buyers/sellers of $1M+ properties, New York's progressive Mansion Tax. Because New York's Department of Taxation and Finance conducts some of the most aggressive residency audits in America, investors planning a move to Florida or Texas must carefully document their 183-day statutory residence and primary home ties.

4 Legal Ways New York Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Harvest capital losses aggressively before year-end to offset both federal and New York State/NYC capital gains dollar-for-dollar.
  • Use Donor-Advised Funds (DAFs) to donate appreciated long-term stock instead of cash—avoiding federal, NY State, and NYC capital gains taxes simultaneously.
  • For NY investment real estate, evaluate a 1031 Tax-Deferred Exchange to defer federal LTCG, 25% depreciation recapture, 3.8% NIIT, and NY State/NYC capital gains taxes in one transaction.
  • Avoid triggering New York's strict 183-day 'statutory resident' trap if you maintain a permanent place of abode in NYC while claiming domicile in another state.

Frequently Asked Questions: New York Capital Gains Tax

Does New York City charge local tax on capital gains?

Yes. NYC residents pay up to 3.876% local city income tax on all realized capital gains on top of New York State (up to 10.9%) and federal taxes.

Does New York offer a lower rate for long-term stocks or crypto?

No. New York taxes short-term and long-term capital gains identically as ordinary income.

Does New York conform to the federal $250k/$500k Section 121 home sale exclusion?

Yes. Because New York starts with Federal Adjusted Gross Income (AGI), gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are also excluded from NY State and NYC income tax.
