How Georgia Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Georgia taxes both short-term and long-term capital gains from stocks, ETFs, crypto, and real estate at its flat 5.39% state income tax rate in 2026 (with scheduled statutory reductions aiming toward 4.99% as state revenue milestones are met) and zero local Atlanta city capital gains tax. Because Georgia begins from Federal Adjusted Gross Income (AGI), Georgia residents automatically inherit federal Tax-Loss Harvesting offsets, $3,000 annual ordinary loss deductions, indefinite carryforwards, Section 121 primary home exclusions ($250k/$500k), and 1031 Exchanges.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Georgia State Tax

On a $100,000 long-term stock or crypto gain for a Single filer earning $150,000 in Atlanta, Georgia adds $5,390 in state tax—bringing total combined tax (Federal 15% + 3.8% NIIT above $200k + GA 5.39%) to roughly $21,530. That saves nearly $4,000 per $100k of gain compared to California and over $5,000 compared to New York City. For retirees age 62+, Georgia's generous retirement income exclusion ($35,000 at ages 62–64; $65,000 at age 65+ per spouse) even covers capital gains, dividends, and rental income!

4 Legal Ways Georgia Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • If age 62+ in Georgia, use Georgia's $35,000–$65,000 per-person retirement income exclusion—it explicitly shields capital gains, interest, and dividends from Georgia's 5.39% tax!
  • Harvest capital losses before December 31 to trim BOTH federal (15%–37%) and Georgia 5.39% taxes simultaneously.
  • Hold winning stocks and crypto past 366 days: since Georgia is a flat 5.39%, the federal LTCG vs. STCG spread is your biggest tax-saving lever.
  • For Georgia rental properties, execute a 1031 Exchange to defer federal LTCG, 25% depreciation recapture, 3.8% NIIT, and Georgia 5.39% tax in one move.

Frequently Asked Questions: Georgia Capital Gains Tax

Does Georgia offer a lower tax rate for long-term capital gains in 2026?

No. Georgia taxes both short-term and long-term capital gains at its flat 5.39% state rate, though seniors age 62+ can shelter $35,000–$65,000/yr of capital gains under Georgia's retirement income exclusion.

Does Georgia conform to the federal $250k/$500k Section 121 home sale exclusion?

Yes. Primary home gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are 100% exempt from Georgia state tax as well.

Does Atlanta charge a local city tax on stock or crypto gains?

No. Atlanta and all Georgia counties charge 0% local capital gains tax.
