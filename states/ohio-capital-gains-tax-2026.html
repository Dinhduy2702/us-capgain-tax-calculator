How Ohio Taxes Capital Gains in 2026

Ohio caps state capital gains taxes at an ultra-low 3.5% in 2026 (0% on the first $26,050 of taxable income, 2.75% up to $100,000, and 3.5% above $100,000)—and just like Pennsylvania, Ohio municipal income taxes (Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati, RITA) do NOT apply to investment capital gains! Because Ohio starts directly from Federal AGI, all federal Tax-Loss Harvesting, $3,000 loss offsets, carryforwards, Section 121 exclusions, and 1031 Exchanges work seamlessly.

Combining Federal LTCG, 3.8% NIIT & Ohio State Tax

On a $100,000 long-term stock or crypto gain in Columbus or Cleveland, Ohio adds roughly ~$3,100–$3,500 in state tax—bringing total combined tax (Federal 15% + 3.8% NIIT + OH) to roughly ~$19,240 (19.2% effective). That makes Ohio one of the most investor-friendly states in the Midwest, saving over $6,000 per $100k of realized gain compared to California.

4 Legal Ways Ohio Investors Cut Capital Gains Tax in 2026

  • Remember Ohio cities (RITA/CCA) only tax earned W-2 wages and business profits—NOT stock, ETF, or crypto capital gains.
  • Use federal Tax-Loss Harvesting and carryforwards freely since Ohio flows directly from Federal AGI.
  • Hold winning investments past 366 days to capture federal 0%/15%/20% LTCG rates alongside Ohio's low 3.5% ceiling.
  • For Ohio rental properties, pair a 1031 Exchange with Ohio's Business Income Deduction planning on operating cash flow.

Frequently Asked Questions: Ohio Capital Gains Tax

Do Columbus, Cleveland, or Cincinnati charge municipal tax on stock or crypto gains?

No. Ohio municipal income taxes apply to earned income (W-2/business), not personal investment capital gains.

What is the maximum Ohio state capital gains tax rate in 2026?

Ohio's top state rate is 3.5% on taxable income above $100,000 (with 0% on the first $26,050 and 2.75% in between).

Does Ohio conform to the federal $250k/$500k home sale exclusion?

Yes. Gains excluded under IRS Section 121 are 100% exempt from Ohio state tax.
